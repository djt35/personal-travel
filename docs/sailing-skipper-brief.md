# Catamaran Skipper's Brief
### For a monohull skipper's first bareboat cat — June 2026
*Applies to both charters: Lefkada (Ionian, Greece) and SW Mallorca (Balearics). Crew: David (skipper, ICC), Lobke, Noah (5).*

---

## 0. The one thing to internalise

**A catamaran does not heel, so it will never *tell* you it's overpowered.**
On a monohull the rail goes down and you ease or round up. A cat just stands up, loads everything to the limit, and accelerates — until something breaks or, in the extreme, the windward hull lifts. Every habit below flows from this: **reef early, by the numbers, not by feel; keep the mainsheet/traveller ready to dump; never drive a cat hard into a gust.**

Cats are *forgiving and stable* in normal conditions (great for a family) and *unforgiving of being over-pressed*. Respect that one boundary and the rest is a comfortable, easy boat.

---

## 1. At handover — learn the boat (don't skip this)

Ask the base for a proper **checkout / familiarisation sail** — say up front it's your first cat. Half a day of own-boat tuition beforehand is money well spent.

Walk the boat and have them show you, hands-on:
- **Reefing system** — single-line or slab? Where are the reef pennants, winches, clutches? Reef *at the mast or from the cockpit*? Do one reef in/out at the dock.
- **Mainsheet + traveller** — the traveller is your primary gust control. Find it, understand the purchase, check it runs freely.
- **Engines** — twin throttle/gear controls, ignition, cooling-water tell-tales, fuel gauges, how to check oil, how to bleed if it cuts.
- **Anchor windlass** — up/down, the breaker, the manual override, how much chain (markings), and **the bridle/snubber** kit.
- **Heads** — electric or manual? Holding tank / seacock discipline (charter killer #1 if blocked).
- **Tanks & power** — water tanks + gauges, battery banks, solar/genset, what runs the fridge overnight, shore power.
- **Bilges & seacocks** — locations, bilge pumps (electric + manual), all seacocks.
- **Safety kit** — liferaft location + painter, grab bag, flares, fire extinguishers + fire blanket (galley), gas shut-off at the bottle, jackline/clip points, danbuoy/LifeSling, first-aid kit.
- **Electronics** — chartplotter, AIS (can you see *and* be seen?), depth, log, wind, autopilot, VHF + **DSC distress button** (program the MMSI / check it's registered).
- **Dinghy + outboard** — launch, fuel, kill-cord, how it stows underway.

**Paperwork to carry:** original **ICC** + **VHF/SRC certificate** (Lobke or you), crew list with all passports.
- *Greece:* you'll also sign a **"competent crew" declaration**; confirm **TEPAI** cruising tax is pre-paid.
- *Balearics:* charter contract + deposit; confirm the **Cabrera permit** if that route is on.

## 2. How a cat differs (the cheat-sheet version)

| | Monohull | Catamaran |
|---|---|---|
| Heel | Heels, warns you | **Stays flat — no warning** |
| Overpower response | Rounds up / spills | Just loads up & accelerates |
| Gust control | Ease sheet | **Dump the traveller** (and sheet) |
| Reefing | When it feels pressed | **Early, by wind speed** |
| Tacking | Spins through | Loses momentum — carry speed |
| Upwind in waves | Slices | **Bridgedeck slams** — ease off |
| Docking | One prop, prop-walk | **Twin engines — pivot on the spot** |
| Steering astern (slow) | Rudder + prop-wash | **Throttles, not the wheel** |
| At anchor | Lies to tide/wind | High windage — **sails around; use a bridle** |
| Beam | ~1 berth | **~1.5–2 berths; call ahead** |
| Draft | Deeper | Shallow — get closer in |

## 3. Sailing her

- **Reef early and by the numbers.** Get the boat-specific table at handover; a typical 40–42 ft charter cat: **1st reef ~16–18 kt true, 2nd reef ~22–24 kt, 3rd/full main down ~28+ kt.** When in doubt, reef — you lose almost no speed and gain huge margin. Easier to shake out than to put in when it's blowing.
- **Traveller is king.** In a gust, **drop the traveller to leeward** to dump power instantly while keeping sail shape — your fastest depower tool. Never cleat the mainsheet hard; keep it ready to run.
- **Apparent wind machine.** Cats are fast, so apparent wind draws forward — you'll sail "tighter" angles than the true wind suggests, and downwind the apparent breeze stays up. Trim to apparent, keep an eye on true.
- **Tacking:** build speed first, bear away a touch, then tack briskly — **don't pinch into it** or you'll stall head-to-wind ("in irons"). In light air or tight water, **back the jib** or **motor-assist** through the tack. No shame in it.
- **Gybing:** controlled only. The mainsheet load is large — sheet in, bring the stern through, ease out the new side; mind the traveller. Rig a preventer for long downwind legs to avoid a crash gybe.
- **Downwind:** a cat's happy place — deep reach or wing-on-wing. Stable, fast, comfortable. Watch for the accidental gybe.
- **Don't bash to weather in a seaway** — the bridgedeck pounds (uncomfortable and slow). Foot off 5–10°, or motor-sail a better angle.

## 4. Under power — the superpower (and the trap)

- **Twin engines = total control.** One ahead, one astern → she **pivots in her own length**. Differential thrust walks her sideways alongside. Most cats need **no bow thruster**.
- **Steer with the throttles, not the wheel,** at low speed. The rudders sit **ahead of / out of the prop-wash**, so the wheel does very little when you're barely moving or going astern. Centre the wheel and drive on the gears.
- **High windage:** the bows blow downwind fast. Always plan your approach **into the wind/current**, and expect to use bursts of engine to hold her.
- **Practise first.** Before the first marina, do figure-eights, stops, and spins in open water until pivoting on the throttles is instinct.

## 5. Berthing & Med-mooring

- **Beam first:** a 40 ft cat is ~7 m wide → ~1.5 berths. **Call the marina on VHF (Ch 9) ahead** and ask for a catamaran/hammerhead berth. Fender **both hulls**.
- **Stern-to (Med-moor) with lazylines** (the norm in Greece, common in the Balearics):
  1. Brief the crew + agree hand signals before the approach.
  2. Reverse in slowly on the throttles, wheel centred.
  3. Get **both stern lines** ashore; walk forward and pick up the **lazyline** from each bow, take up tight to hold the bows off the quay.
  4. Adjust with short engine bursts; the two widely-spaced engines hold you straight beautifully.
- **Anchor-and-back** (where there are no lazylines): drop the hook well out, back down paying chain, two stern lines ashore, snub the chain.
- **Finger pontoons** (some Balearic marinas): easy — spring off, walk her in on the engines.
- Two sterns = **easy, safe boarding** for Noah and the shopping.

## 6. Anchoring (you'll do this most nights)

- **Find a clear sand patch.**
  - *Balearics:* **anchoring on Posidonia seagrass is illegal — fines €3,001–€200,000, satellite/patrol-enforced (2025).** Use the free **Donia / Posidonia Maps** app to find legal sand, or take a buoy. This is non-negotiable.
  - *Greece:* weed-over-mud is common — it may take 2–3 goes to set; drop on a sand/mud patch and dig in.
- **Scope:** 4:1 minimum, **5:1+** for an overnight or breeze. Cats are light → they need good scope and a well-set hook.
- **Rig the bridle/snubber every time.** A line from each bow to the chain takes the load off the windlass, **stops the cat sailing around**, spreads the load across both hulls and kills the snatch. This is the single most important cat-anchoring habit.
- **Set it:** reverse firmly (both engines), watch a transit ashore, then **snorkel-check** the hook in clear water — quick and reassuring.
- **Swing & watch:** cats swing to the *wind*, not the tide (Med is tideless) — they range about more than monos. Pick room clear of swimmers, buoys, rocks and other boats. Set a **drag/anchor alarm** on the plotter or your phone.
- **Line ashore** in tight Ionian coves: anchor + long line to a rock/tree to stop swinging — carry a 30–50 m line + shackle.

## 7. Daily routine

- **Sail mornings, anchor by ~14:00.** The Med sea breeze (Ionian *maïstro* NW / Balearic *Embat* S–SW) is calm at dawn, builds F3–5 by afternoon, dies at dusk. Plan legs for the morning, be settled before it peaks.
- **Forecast every morning:** *Greece* — Poseidon/HCMR + Windy/Windguru; *Balearics* — AEMET + Windy. Have a **go / no-go** and a **fallback anchorage** for the day's wind direction.
- **Manage consumables:** top up water and fuel at any marina stop (small islands often have none); check bilges daily; watch battery state overnight (fridge is the big draw).
- **Sun & hydration** — strong June sun; hats, shade, water, especially for Noah.

## 8. Safety

- **The no-heel rule (again):** reef before squalls, never drive hard into a gust, keep the mainsheet free to dump. A reefed, slightly slow cat is a safe cat.
- **Squalls / building wind:** reef down, **bear away and slow down**, ride it out; don't try to point through it.
- **MOB:** the cat is a stable platform and stops easily under power — but the **helm is high and forward, so a person in the water is hard to see**. Brief everyone: shout, point continuously, throw the danbuoy/LifeSling, hit **MOB** on the plotter, start engines, drop sail, return under power. Practise once early in the week.
- **Kids (Noah, 5):**
  - **His own lifejacket** (CE 50/100N, crotch strap + grab handle) — bring it; charter cats rarely have child sizes. **On whenever on deck or in the dinghy.**
  - "**One hand for the boat**"; clip to a jackline in any breeze; brief him on the **trampoline rules** and the high boom.
  - **Swimming = engines OFF, ladder down, headcount in and out.**
- **Gas:** off at the bottle when not cooking. **Fire:** know the extinguishers + galley fire blanket. **Heads:** nothing down them that you didn't eat first.
- **VHF:** monitor **Ch 16**; marinas **Ch 9**. Distress = **DSC red button** then voice MAYDAY on 16.
  - *Greece:* Coastguard **108** or **112**. *Spain:* **112** / Salvamento Marítimo on Ch 16.
- **Liferaft, grab bag, flares** — know where they are and how to deploy on day one.

## 10. Trip-specific add-ons

**Lefkada / Ionian (Greece)**
- Lazyline Med-mooring is the norm; weed holding (set carefully); line-ashore in tight coves.
- No fuel at Kastos/Kalamos/Fiskardo/Kioni — **leave Lefkas with full tanks/water.**
- **Lefkas canal swing bridge** opens ~hourly, one-way convoy — verify times with the marina.
- ICC **+ competent-crew declaration**; confirm **TEPAI** pre-paid.

**SW Mallorca (Balearics)**
- **Posidonia anchoring ban** — sand-only, Donia app, or buoys. Biggest local pitfall.
- **Tramuntana** (hard N wind) makes the N coast a dangerous lee shore — your SW route sits in its lee; still check forecasts.
- Busy **ferry & fast-charter traffic** in Palma Bay — sharp lookout, AIS on.
- **Yellow buoys = swim zones:** no entry, speed limits near beaches.
- All-weather boltholes: Port d'Andratx, Port Adriano, Puerto Portals.
