⚓ Skipper's notes

Wind: June = SW sea-breeze (Llebeig/Garbí), F2–4, building early-afternoon and dying at dusk. Sail mornings — the westbound leg to Sant Elm is upwind against the Llebeig if you leave it late. Rare N Tramuntana brings katabatic gusts off the SW hills — check the forecast.
Sant Elm / Sa Dragonera: the beach anchorage is open to W/SW swell — in a Llebeig, take the sheltered Cala Lladó buoys on Dragonera or drop back to Port d'Andratx marina (5 NM). Dragonera is a marine reserve — anchoring restricted, use the buoys; landing to hike the lighthouse trails is free, no services (bring water).
Fuel/water: Port d'Andratx base, Port Adriano, Puerto Portals & Palma (Club de Mar) all have fuel + water. The coves don't.
Provisioning: big shop in Andratx town before departure; SW resorts have small shops only; Palma = full supermarkets.
Marinas: the SW is Mallorca's most upmarket strip — Andratx, Puerto Portals & Port Adriano berths are pricey. Anchor the coves; limit marina nights to the base + Palma.
Forecast: AEMET + Windy / Windguru. The Llebeig builds most afternoons.
VHF: monitor Ch 16; marinas Ch 9; Salvamento Marítimo (coastguard) 112 / Ch 16.
🛟 Child lifejacket: charter cats rarely carry child sizes — bring Noah's own (CE 50/100N, crotch strap + grab handle) and pre-request one in writing. Lifejacket on whenever he's on deck or in the dinghy.
Pre-request: SUP/kayak · snorkel gear · extra fenders · autopilot · full bimini & sun-shade (strong June sun).

⚓ Skipper's briefing

1 · Easy sailing — Bay of Palma

The Balearics are non-tidal, line-of-sight, with few off-lying dangers — among the easiest Med cruising grounds. Your SW + Bay-of-Palma loop is the gentlest corner of it: hops under ~20 NM, deep clear water, sheltered calas (Portals Vells, Santa Ponsa, Camp de Mar, Illetes).
The daily rhythm: glassy mornings → motor / light sail; the Embat sea-breeze fills S/SW to F3–4 by early afternoon → dies at dusk. Sail mornings, anchor by ~14:00, swim all afternoon. The bay sits in the island's lee, shielded from the N Tramuntana.

2 · Catamaran ≠ monohull (read this)

The #1 trap for a monohull skipper: a cat doesn't heel, so it won't tell you it's overpowered. On a mono you feel the rail dip; on a cat you can be badly over-canvassed and not notice until it's dangerous. Reef early, by the numbers (apparent wind / the base's reefing table) — not by feel.
Docking = superpower: twin engines 4–5 m apart spin her on the spot — steer with the throttles, not the wheel, at low speed. Rudders sit ahead of the props, so little prop-wash bite astern. No bow-thruster needed.
Tacking: a cat bleeds momentum through the turn — carry speed, bear off to build it, don't pinch; in light air or tight water motor through the tack. Wider tacking angles than a mono.
Windage & beam: high, light, big profile → she sails around at anchor and gets shoved in close quarters. Use an anchor bridle (spreads load, stops yawing). A 40-ft cat is ~7 m wide — takes ~1.5 berths, so call ahead for a catamaran berth (fees ~1.5×).
Upwind in a sea: the bridgedeck slams — slow and uncomfortable; ease off the wind, don't bash to weather.
✓ Prep: book a proper cat checkout / handover sail with the base, or a half-day multihull conversion before you go. This applies equally to the Lefkada cat — worth doing once either way.

3 · Dangers & local rules

Posidonia seagrass — big-money rule: anchoring on protected seagrass is illegal — fines €3,001–€200,000, with 2025 real-time satellite/patrol enforcement (18 boats). Anchor only on sand (light patches) or use buoys. Check the free Donia / Posidonia Maps app for legal spots.
Tramuntana (N/NW): arrives hard off the mountains — dangerous lee shore on the N/NW coast (few harbours). Your SW route is in its lee, but watch the forecast for katabatic gusts off the hills.
Embat: the afternoon S/SW sea-breeze (F3–5) turns westbound legs upwind — make harbour before it peaks.
Lee shore: a cala safe in N wind is untenable in S/E swell. Pick the anchorage to match the day's wind; keep an all-weather marina (Andratx, Port Adriano, Puerto Portals) as fallback.
Traffic & swimmers: Palma Bay is busy with ferries & fast charter boats — sharp lookout, monitor VHF 16. Yellow buoys = swim zones: no entry, strict speed limits near beaches.
Crowded calas: peak coves fill fast & boats drag in afternoon gusts — set the hook well, snorkel-check it, keep swinging room clear of swimmers, moorings & rocks.
🛟 Strong June sun — hats, shade & hydration for Noah; lifejacket on deck always.
