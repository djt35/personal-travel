Cyclades vs Lefkada — read first

Meltemi: the Cyclades are the windiest part of Greece. Early September the N/NW Meltemi can still blow F5–7 for days, with big open crossings and short steep seas. For a family with Noah (5) and a skipper new to catamarans, this is materially harder than the Ionian.
✓ This route mitigates it: a tight Small Cyclades loop (Paros–Naxos–Koufonisia–Schinoussa) that hugs the lee of Naxos, keeps crossings short and offers bail-out anchorages. Do the exposed legs at dawn before the wind builds.
Recommendation: keep Lefkada as the primary family choice (gentler, more forgiving for a cat checkout). Choose the Cyclades only if you specifically want the iconic white-and-blue Aegean and you get a settled forecast window — and strongly consider a skippered cat here rather than bareboat.

⚓ Skipper's notes (Aegean)

Wind: Meltemi from N/NW; strongest midday–evening, eases overnight. Sail early, be in by 14:00. Watch acceleration zones between islands (gusts +15–20 kt).
Ground: many Cyclades harbours are exposed or ferry-washed; carry a generous scope and prefer laid moorings / well-sheltered bays. Naxos, Koufonisia and Schinoussa are the calmest here.
Ferries: big fast ferries at Paros & Naxos — keep clear of fairways, expect wake.
Bail-outs: Naxos marina and the Small Cyclades give lee shelter in most Meltemi; abandon southbound plans (Ios/Amorgos) if it's blowing.
🐈 Cat checkout essential — David has only skippered monohulls. 🛟 Bring Noah's own child lifejacket.
