## Bottom line

1. **Start in Cape Town, then go to the coast.** A rain system crosses the southern Cape on **Sun 1 and Mon 2 Nov**:
   - Garden Route: 92–98% of ensemble members show rain on Sun 1, and 75–82% on Mon 2.
   - Totals: 11–18 mm at Wilderness, Knysna, Plett and Tsitsikamma.
   - Cape Town and the Winelands stay **dry**, with only 12–18% of members showing rain.
2. **Tue 3 to Thu 5 is the best window everywhere.** It is dry along the whole N2 and winds are light.
   - **Thu 5 is a hot berg-wind day** on the Garden Route: 30–33 °C at the coast and 38 °C at Oudtshoorn. Make it a beach day.
3. **A second, weaker front arrives Fri 6 to Sun 8**, bringing westerly showers.
   - Garden Route on Sat 7: 49–57% of members show rain, 3–10 mm, and highs of only 17–21 °C.
   - Cape Town on Sat 7: about 31% of members show rain.
   - **Addo/Eastern Cape stays driest at the east end:** the main forecast gives 0 mm on Sat 7, with 35% of ensemble members showing rain.
4. **Cape Town's real weather risk is wind, not rain.** The SE "Cape Doctor" gusts at 58–67 km/h on Sat 31, Sun 1, Mon 2, Thu 5, Sat 7 and Sun 8, so the **cable car may close**.
   - **Calm days for Table Mountain: Tue 3 (gusts 43) and especially Wed 4 (gusts 28, 28 °C).**

How to read the table:
- **The Garden Route has the most rain days (26–30%)** because it gets rain all year. Expect passing showers rather than washed-out days, apart from frontal days like Sun 1.
- **The Garden Route has the warmest sea (about 20 °C)**: Plett and Wilderness are the swimmable beaches. The Cape Town Atlantic side (13 °C) is for looking at, and False Bay (17 °C) is borderline.
- **The Garden Route has the least wind.** Cape Town is sunnier and drier, but blustery on about 1 day in 3.
- **The Karoo (Oudtshoorn) is the dry, hot inland escape** when the coast is grey: 27 °C average and only 17% rain days.
- Sunshine is about 12 hours a day almost everywhere. **UV index is 8–9** on clear days, so Noah needs SPF 50+, a hat and a rash vest.

### El Niño angle
WMO (Aug 2026) puts El Niño as firmly established and strengthening, peaking late 2026. In the 20-year record, early-November El Niño years (2006, 2009, 2015, 2023) were only marginally wetter in Cape Town and Hermanus (+5–8 percentage points of rain days). Along the Garden Route they were no different. **This signal is weak; don't plan around it.** Separately, SAWS reported a record-dry July in the SW Cape. That has no bearing on November day-to-day weather, but it does mean possible water-saving notices.

## Wildlife and season notes
- **Southern right whales are late-season but still present**. Hermanus guides call Sept–Nov near-guaranteed. Plett has southern rights until about November, then the humpbacks arrive. Bryde's whales and dolphins are in Plett all year. Boat trips from Plett or Gansbaai are the best bet in early November.
- Days are long: sunrise is about 05:40 and sunset about 19:30 in Cape Town.
- Since Belgium went back to winter time on 25 Oct, **South Africa is 1 hour ahead of Belgium** (no jet lag).

## Weather-led shape (to confirm against flights and return date)
- **Sat 31 Oct to Tue 3 Nov: Cape Town / Winelands (3 nights).**
  - Sun 1 and Mon 2: Winelands and the Boulders penguins (Simon's Town), which are sheltered from the SE wind.
  - Tue 3 morning: Table Mountain, or Wed 4 if staying an extra night.
- **Tue 3: transfer east on the dry day.** Either fly CPT→George (about 1 hour), or drive the N2 (about 6 hours) with a whale stop at Hermanus and lunch at Swellendam.
- **Tue 3 to Sat 7: Garden Route, based in Plett or Knysna (4 nights).**
  - Wed 4: Robberg / Knysna Heads.
  - Thu 5: beach day.
  - Fri 6: Tsitsikamma suspension bridge, before the next front.
- **Sat 7 to Sun 8: either Addo (driest option for that weekend; fly home from Gqeberha/PE) or back to Cape Town.**
- **Avoid:** being on the Garden Route on Sun 1/Mon 2, and booking a fixed-time Table Mountain slot on a Cape Doctor day.
