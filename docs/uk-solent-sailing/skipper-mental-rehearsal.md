# Solent Bareboat — Skipper's Mental-Rehearsal Brief

*David + Lobke · ~37 ft monohull · Route A (Solent + Isle of Wight taster), midweek 3–4 days · 2027 season · fair-weather, inside sheltered water. Prepared 21 Jul 2026. Planning aid only — nothing booked.*

---

## The honest confidence check (read this first)

You have everything the job needs: **Day Skipper (Practical, Sail) + VHF SRC**, you're **fairly current on monohulls**, and Lobke is a **Day-Skipper-trained, competent second pair of hands**. Route A never leaves categorised sheltered water, never rounds the Needles, and every leg is a 2–4 hour hop with a safe harbour always within reach. In a settled SW F3–4 this is a **relaxed cruise, not a test**. The rehearsal below isn't because it's hard — it's so the handful of Solent-specific set-pieces are *automatic* and you enjoy it rather than problem-solve it.

**The five set-pieces worth chair-flying until they're reflex:**
1. Leaving and returning to the home berth, two-handed.
2. Reading the Solent tide so you always carry a fair stream and hit the tidal "gates" (harbour entrances) at the right height.
3. Crossing the shipping channel / precautionary area cleanly (big ships, ferries, the Bramble Bank).
4. Coming alongside a visitor berth and picking up a mooring buoy, two-handed.
5. Man-overboard with only two of you (the one real "what if" — mostly a *prevention* drill).

---

## How to mentally rehearse (chair-flying)

Fighter pilots call it chair-flying; do the same in an armchair the week before.

- **Run it in real time, eyes shut.** Don't summarise — *narrate the actions in sequence*: "engine on, checks, brief Lobke, she takes the bow line, I check astern, tide's setting me off the pontoon so I let it…" Speaking it aloud fixes the order.
- **Rehearse the hands, not just the plan.** Feel yourself putting the engine astern, walking the midships line to a cleat, easing the spring. Motor memory is what you're building.
- **Always rehearse the failure and *your response*, then stop.** Engine cuts on the approach → I bear away into clear water, sort it or sail/anchor. Don't rehearse disaster without the recovery — you're training the calm reaction, not the fear.
- **Pre-load the numbers.** On the day you'll have real tide times and a forecast; rehearsing with the *template* at the end means the real planning is just filling blanks.

## Brush-up before the trip (a rusty-corner shortlist)

None of this is new to you — just the bits that go first when you're out of practice:

- **Springing off** a pontoon (bow spring / midships spring) when wind or tide pins you on.
- **Ferry-gliding** and the **midships-line-first** alongside technique for short-handed berthing.
- **Picking up a mooring buoy** under engine into wind/tide, and **anchoring** (scope 4:1 chain, check it holds).
- **A tidal height calc for a secondary port** — you'll want it for the **Beaulieu bar** and **Newtown** (both shallow). Rule-of-twelfths in your head is enough.
- **A two-handed MOB** recovery — quick-stop under engine, and the crane/recovery problem when you're the only one aboard who can pull.
- **VHF working practice**: marina calls on their channel, and who runs the harbour (Cowes, QHM Portsmouth).

## The rehearsal — phase by phase

### A. Handover & pre-departure (at the marina)
Visualise the boat handover walk-round with the charter company: seacocks, bilge, engine (oil, water, belt, raw-water strainer), fuel level, gas, battery/isolators, nav lights, VHF & DSC (MMSI registered), GPS/plotter, reefing lines, kicker/vang, winches, anchor & windlass, danbuoy/horseshoe/throwline, lifejackets fitted to each of you, flares/first-aid location, seacock & through-hull count, dinghy. **Get the company's emergency number and the boat's limits (Solent categorised area only) in writing.**

Then, *before slipping*: check the **forecast** (Met Office Inshore Waters *Selsey Bill to Lyme Regis*, plus the *Wight/Portland* shipping areas), note **HW/LW times and stream direction**, write a one-line **passage plan** per leg (course, hazards, ETA, tidal gate), brief Lobke (below), and log a plan/ETA. Reef decision made *now*: SW F4+ → one slab in before you leave.

### B. Leaving the berth (two-handed)
The order that keeps it calm:
1. **Read the forces**: which way is the **wind** pushing, which way is the **tide** setting on the berth, and your boat's **prop-walk** direction astern (find it early — a short burst astern in the berth tells you).
2. **Set the lines to work *with* those forces.** If tide/wind will carry the bow off, let it — slip everything but a stern line and peel out backwards. If pinned on, rig a **midships or bow spring** and motor gently against it to swing the stern (or bow) out, then slip.
3. **Fenders and lines ready to recover**; Lobke on the line you'll keep last, you on the helm.
4. **Look astern and around before any movement** — Solent marinas are busy.
- *Lead-pick note (Solent Yacht Charter / Haslar, Portsmouth):* your first move after leaving Haslar is the **Portsmouth Harbour entrance** — narrow, strong tide, commercial + ferry traffic. Keep to the **Small Boat Channel** on the western side, monitor **QHM Portsmouth Ch 11**, and never impede a big ship or the Gosport ferry. If you charter from **Hamble (Nomad)** or **Lymington**, the exit is a gentle river motor instead — easier, but you then cross more open Solent.

### C. Solent transit skills (the bit that makes you look like you know the place)
- **Tide is your engine.** The Solent runs hard (2–3 kn in the central channel, more at the edges). *Plan every leg to carry a fair tide* — an hour's wait for the turn can be worth a knot-and-a-half. Remember the Solent's **long stand / double high water** — the "flat" top of the tide gives you a wide window for shallow entrances.
- **The shipping channel & precautionary area.** Big commercial ships use the buoyed Thorn/main channel into Southampton and have a **Moving Prohibited Zone** (small craft keep well clear — ~1,000 m ahead / 100 m around vessels >150 m). Plan to **cross the channel at right angles, promptly, in a gap**, not to loiter in it. Watch **Red Funnel / Wightlink ferries** (they don't stop for you) and the **Cowes chain ferry** across the Medina.
- **The Bramble Bank** sits mid-Solent and dries near low water — a cricket match is played on it at LW springs. Know where it is; the marks and the channel keep you off it.
- **Sail plan**: SW F3–4 is ideal; **reef early** and shake out later if over-canvassed. Afternoon sea-breeze can add a force.
- **Two-up roles**: no formal watches needed on 2–4 h hops — just split **helm** and **nav/lookout**, swap when you like, keep a mug of tea going. Lifejackets on; clip to jackstays if it's lively or anyone's on the foredeck.

### D. Leg-by-leg pilotage (rehearse each arrival)
*(Route A classic flow from an eastern/central base; reverse legs if you're Lymington-based.)*

- **Day 1 → Cowes.** The easy shake-down cross. Call **Cowes Yacht Haven / Shepards** on VHF for a berth; mind the **chain ferry** and racing fleets in the Medina. Overnight in town — pubs, chandlers, the works.
- **Day 2 → Yarmouth.** Pretty, *small, and busy* — **book ahead** in season; you may **raft up** or take a walk-ashore pontoon. Watch the **Wightlink ferry** turning in the approach. Sheltered, lovely evening.
- **Day 3 → Beaulieu (Buckler's Hard) or Newtown Creek.**
  - **Beaulieu**: cross the **shallow bar at the entrance** near/after half-tide, follow the **withy/beacon-marked channel** up the river to Buckler's Hard marina — gorgeous, wooded, calm. *This is your tidal-height set-piece — do the calc.*
  - **Newtown**: National Trust, secluded, **buoys or anchor**, shallow and partly drying — settled weather only, and a tidal-height check before you commit. The quiet night of the trip.
- **Final day → back to base.** Fair-tide leg home, refuel to full, berth (Phase E in reverse), hand back.

### E. Berthing, buoys & anchoring, two-handed
- **Alongside (short-handed):** fenders + lines rigged *early*, approach **slow, into the stronger of wind/tide**. The reliable two-up trick: **get one midships line onto a cleat first**, motor gently ahead against it — the boat pins herself alongside and you both step off to make fast bow/stern at leisure.
- **Mooring buoy:** approach **into wind/tide**, aim to stop with the buoy at the shrouds; Lobke picks up with the boathook and leads the strop through the bow fairlead to a cleat. Miss it? Calmly circle and re-approach — no drama.
- **Anchor:** pick your spot for **swinging room and holding**, drop with the bow into wind/tide, pay out **~4× depth in chain**, snub, and take a **transit ashore** to confirm it's holding before you relax.

### F. Contingencies — rehearse the response, then move on
- **Man overboard (two-handed) — the one to really drill.** *Prevention first:* lifejackets always on, **clip to jackstays** on deck and at night. If it happens: **shout, hit the DSC MOB / mark GPS, throw the danbuoy+horseshoe, keep eyes locked on them, one of you never stops pointing.** Recover under engine (quick-stop): come back, stop alongside to leeward, engine to neutral near the casualty, get a line on them, then the hard part — **recovery with only one puller** (halyard + winch, boarding ladder, bight of line as a step). Rehearse this until the *sequence* is automatic; it's the difference between a scare and a tragedy.
- **Engine failure:** you have sails — **bear away into clear water and sea room**, then diagnose (fuel? air in the system? raw-water/weed?). No fix → **sail to a buoy or anchor**. Never drift into the channel.
- **Fog / poor viz:** slow down, **nav lights + sound signals**, hug the edge of the channel out of the ships' water, use the plotter/AIS, consider anchoring in shallow safe water to wait it out.
- **Aground:** Solent is mostly **soft mud**. *Don't gun it.* Tide **rising?** — you'll likely float off; kedge or wait. **Falling?** — act fast: engine astern, **heel her** (crew weight/boom out to reduce draught), motor off the way you came.
- **Seasick / off-colour crew:** short hops, keep them on deck on the helm, hydrate; it's rarely serious inside the Solent.

### G. Return & hand-back
Refuel **full-to-full**, berth calmly (you've now done it a few times), pump the bilge, tidy, and walk the boat back with the company. Log the trip. Debrief with Lobke over a pint: what flowed, what you'd tweak — that's how the *next* charter (Route B, the full IoW circumnavigation) gets easier.

## Lobke's two-handed crew brief (her jobs)
Keep it simple and pre-agreed so nobody's shouting:
- **Lines & fenders:** which line she takes leaving/arriving, and the "midships-first" plan alongside.
- **Foredeck:** anchor/buoy pick-up, headsail changes/reefing on the foredeck — **always clipped on**.
- **Lookout & ferries:** she calls traffic and the chain ferry while you helm the channel crossing.
- **MOB:** her single job if *you* go over is **point and don't lose you** while she works the DSC/engine — worth one dry run.
- **Comfort:** she runs the kettle and the pilotage book; you run the boat.
